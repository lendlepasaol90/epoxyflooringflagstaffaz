Flagstaff's business calendar runs on seasons. Ski traffic in winter, tourists and Grand Canyon visitors in summer, students coming and going with the university year, and quieter shoulder seasons in between. A floor project has to fit somewhere in that rhythm without costing you your best weeks.

Resin flooring gives commercial spaces a seamless surface that stands up to washdown, chemicals, snow and constant foot traffic, and the right system can be installed in a weekend or phased around operations. We work with breweries, restaurants, retailers, clinics, offices and lodging in and around town, and every project starts with understanding how your space works.

Brewery and taproom floors

Flagstaff's brewing scene puts some of the toughest demands on any floor. Production areas see hot caustic cleaners, sugary wort, acidic sanitizers, constant water and heavy equipment. Floors slope to trench drains and are hosed down daily. Many original concrete floors in older buildings were never designed for that.

For brew houses and cellars, we often recommend urethane cement systems, which tolerate thermal shock from hot washdown and resist the chemicals used in brewing. Texture is set for safe footing on a wet floor, and the coating is carried into drains and up walls as a cove so there is nowhere for water and grime to hide.

Taprooms need something different: a floor that looks good, handles spilled beer and constant chairs, and cleans up fast at closing. A decorative flake or solid system with a tough topcoat suits most front-of-house spaces.

Why resin floors suit commercial spaces

Seamless cleaning. No grout lines or seams for dirt, bacteria and water to collect in. Coved edges let mops and squeegees reach every corner.

Chemical resistance. Topcoats can be chosen for your specific cleaners, sanitizers and spills.

Zone-by-zone texture. More grip in wet and entry areas, smoother finishes where appearance matters.

Durability under traffic. Resin floors handle foot traffic, carts, chairs and equipment far better than vinyl or sealed bare concrete.

Lower maintenance. No waxing or stripping. Clean regularly and refresh the topcoat in high-wear areas every few years.

Scheduling around your season

We plan commercial projects so the floor does not cost you your busiest weeks.

Shoulder-season installs. Many businesses schedule floors during spring or fall lulls, when traffic is lighter.

Overnight and weekend work. Fast-curing systems allow smaller spaces to be finished between closing and opening, or over a weekend.

Phasing. Larger spaces can be split so part of the business stays open while we work on another area.

Containment. Dust control and low-odor products keep neighboring businesses and upstairs tenants comfortable.

Before the first grinder starts, your team has a calendar showing which areas close, for how long and when they reopen.

Working in downtown and older buildings

Many Flagstaff businesses occupy older buildings downtown and along Route 66. Those spaces often have concrete that has been patched, painted or covered several times, uneven floors and drains that were added later. Some have basements or below-grade areas with moisture to manage.

We are used to working with those conditions and with landlords and neighbors in shared buildings. We coordinate access and noise, keep dust contained and plan for the realities of a building that might be a century old. For newer commercial spaces near the airport, east Flagstaff and along Milton Road, the focus shifts to scheduling, durability and appearance.

Why the installer matters for a business

A failed commercial floor means more than a cosmetic problem. It can mean a slip hazard, a failed inspection or another closure. The details that prevent that include moisture testing, the right system for your chemicals and temperatures, correct detailing at drains and walls, and texture in the right places. We take the time to get those right up front, so the floor quietly does its job for years.

What shapes the price of a commercial floor

No two commercial projects price the same, but the drivers are consistent. Removing old flooring, especially tile or glued-down vinyl, can be a large share of the work. Slab condition and moisture matter, particularly in older downtown buildings. Brewery and kitchen systems with urethane cement, coves and drain detailing cost more per square foot than a front-of-house flake floor. Overnight or phased schedules add planning and crew time.

We break those items out line by line so you can see the trade-offs. Often the smartest budget puts the heavy system where water and chemicals are, and a simpler, attractive finish everywhere else.

Keeping a commercial floor in shape

Staff training is the cheapest maintenance there is. We leave a short guide covering which cleaners suit the topcoat, how to handle spills and what to avoid. Entry mats and grates do a huge amount of work in winter by trapping cinders before they spread across the floor.

Expect the busiest paths, such as the line from the door to the counter or the route between kitchen and dish pit, to show wear first. A maintenance topcoat in those zones every few years keeps the whole floor even and takes far less time than the original installation.

Commercial flooring questions

Can you install while we stay open?

Often, by phasing the work or using overnight and weekend windows with fast-curing systems. We plan the schedule with you before starting.

What floor do you recommend for a brewery?

For production areas with hot washdown, urethane cement is usually the best fit. Taprooms often suit a flake or solid resin system with a durable topcoat.

Will a resin floor be slippery when wet?

We set texture zone by zone. Wet areas and entries get more slip resistance, while dry areas can be smoother for easy cleaning.

Do you coat over existing tile or VCT?

We generally remove existing flooring and coat the concrete beneath, which gives a much more reliable bond.

How long will the floor last?

With the right system and routine cleaning, many years. High-wear lanes can be refreshed with a maintenance topcoat instead of a full replacement.

Can you work with our landlord?

We do this often in downtown buildings. Property managers usually want technical sheets for the products, a timeline and a plan for dust and odor, and we prepare all three.

Can different parts of our space have different finishes?

Yes, and it is common. A brewery might have urethane cement in the brew house, a decorative flake floor in the taproom and a simple sealed floor in dry storage, all with clean transitions between them.

Do you handle trench drains in brewery floors?

Yes. Trench and floor drains are detailed carefully, with the system carried into the drain edges and the floor sloped where needed. Drains are one of the most common failure points, so they get extra attention.

Can you match our brand colors?

Solid color systems can be tinted close to most brand colors, and flake blends can be chosen to complement them. We test samples in your space before committing.
