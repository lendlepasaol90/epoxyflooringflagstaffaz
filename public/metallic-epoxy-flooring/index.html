Metallic epoxy floors with movement and depth

A metallic floor looks like it was poured from stone, water or ice. Pigments suspended in clear resin are moved by hand, then frozen in place as the floor cures, so no two are ever the same.

Metallic epoxy is the most artistic system we install. Instead of a flat color or a scatter of chips, the floor is made of fine reflective pigments floating in clear resin. While it is wet, we push, pull and lift those pigments with rollers, trowels and brushes. As it levels and cures, the pattern settles into soft clouds, veins or currents that shift as light moves across the room.

In a Flagstaff home, that can mean a basement family room that glows like a frozen lake, a great room with the warm depth of polished bronze, or a gallery floor that feels like slate. This page covers what to expect, where metallic works best and how the climate here affects the process.

What sets a metallic floor apart

Most floor coatings are about covering concrete. Metallic is about depth. Because you are looking into a clear layer of resin with reflective particles suspended at different levels, the floor appears to have dimension, more like a polished stone slab than a painted surface.

That effect depends on three things. The background tone, set by a tinted base coat under the metallic layer. The pigments themselves, which may be pearl, silver, copper, bronze or tinted micas. And the technique used to move them, which controls whether the finish reads as soft and cloudy, sharply veined or flowing like water.

Because the final pattern forms as the resin levels, a metallic floor can be guided but not fully controlled. That unpredictability is the appeal. It also means you should expect your floor to share the character of a sample rather than match it exactly.

How we create a metallic floor

Metallic floors take more preparation and planning than any other system.

Design first. We look at photos you like, talk through color and movement and bring sample boards. We are honest about which effects are realistic.

Flat, sound concrete. Metallic resin highlights every low spot and patch line, so the slab is ground flat and repaired carefully.

Primer and tinted base. These seal the concrete and set the background tone the metallics will play against.

The pour. The metallic layer is mixed, poured and worked across the whole floor in one continuous session, so there are no visible seams.

Topcoat. A clear protective layer in gloss or satin adds scratch resistance and, where sunlight reaches, UV stability.

Setting the right expectations

We want you to love your floor, so we are upfront. Metallic floors are unique and cannot be replicated exactly. Gloss finishes show dust, footprints and fine scratches more than satin or flake. Spot repairs to the decorative layer are difficult to blend invisibly. And metallic is not the best choice for a working garage full of tools and grit.

For the right room, none of that matters, and the result is a floor people stop to look at. For a mudroom or workshop, a flake system will serve you better, and we will say so.

Cold rooms, altitude sun and metallic resin

Temperature has a big effect on metallic work. Resin thickens in a cold room, which changes how pigments move and how the floor levels. Many Flagstaff basements and cabins are cool for much of the year, so we warm the space beforehand and keep it steady during the pour and cure.

Sunlight is the other factor. At this elevation, UV through big windows is strong enough to shift the color of unprotected epoxy over time. In sunlit rooms we specify UV-stable topcoats to keep the floor's true tones.

Finally, think about grit. Even in a living space, cinders and sand get tracked in during winter. Mats at doors and a satin topcoat help a metallic floor stay looking its best.

Why metallic needs an experienced hand

Metallic epoxy is one of the least forgiving coatings to install. Working time is limited, the entire floor has to be handled in one session, and mistakes such as roller marks, uneven mixing or dust in the surface are permanent. The pattern relies on technique that only comes with practice.

Hiring a crew with metallic experience is the difference between a floor that looks like polished stone and one that looks like a spill. Ask any installer to show you sample boards and finished floors before you commit.

Living with and caring for a metallic floor

Dust mop regularly and damp mop with a neutral cleaner. Put felt pads on furniture legs and use entry mats to keep grit off the surface. Avoid dragging heavy items. If the topcoat dulls in high-traffic paths after years of use, it can be lightly abraded and recoated, renewing the shine without touching the decorative layer.

Pricing reflects the extra layers and labor. Size, slab condition, the number of colors and the topcoat all affect cost, and we quote each project individually after seeing the space.

Choosing the right room for a metallic floor

The best metallic floors are in rooms where people linger and where the light changes through the day. Basements with a mix of lamp light and window light, great rooms with big views, galleries and tasting rooms all show off the depth of the finish.

Rooms that see a lot of grit, such as mudrooms right off a gravel driveway, are less suited, because fine scratches build up faster on gloss. If you love the look but have a busy entry, consider a metallic floor in the main room and a fine flake in the entry, joined with a clean transition.

Metallic floor questions

Can I see examples before deciding?

Yes. We bring sample boards in several palettes to the estimate and can show you a range of effects so you can choose with confidence.

Will my floor look exactly like the sample?

It will share the palette and general style, but every metallic floor forms its own pattern as it levels. Think of the sample as a guide, not a copy.

Is a metallic floor suitable for a basement?

Basements are one of the best places for it, provided moisture levels are tested and managed. A moisture-mitigating primer goes under the decorative layers when needed.

Does metallic work in a garage?

In a garage used mainly for parking and display, yes. In a working garage with tools, salt and cinders, a flake system is more practical.

How long does it take?

Plan on most of a week from first grind to moving furniture back for an average basement, a little longer if the room is cold and we need to bring it up to temperature first.

Can metallic go over radiant floor heat?

Often, with the heat turned down for installation and brought back gradually afterward. We confirm product suitability for heated slabs before quoting.

Is a metallic floor slippery?

Gloss metallic floors are smooth, so they can be slick with water or snow on them. In living spaces that is rarely an issue; near entries we recommend a satin topcoat with a fine texture additive.

Can you include a border or two colors?

Simple borders, color zones and transitions between palettes are possible on many projects. We plan these during the design conversation so the layout suits the room.

How do I clean a metallic floor?

Dust mop often and damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner. Skip harsh chemicals and abrasive pads, and use mats at doors to catch winter grit.
