Epoxy and resin floors for high-country homes and businesses

We coat concrete for people who live and work at altitude. That means garages that see snowmelt for half the year, cabins that sit empty through the cold months, breweries and shops downtown, and warehouses out along I-40. Each one asks something different from a floor.

Our job is to figure out what that is. We look at the slab first: how old it is, whether it is scaling or cracked, whether it holds moisture, and whether the space is heated. Then we recommend a system that will actually hold up, whether that is a garage floor coating with a textured flake finish, a fast-curing polyaspartic build for a cold-season install, a flexible polyurea base for a cracked slab or a seamless commercial floor that stays easy to clean.

Every estimate is free and done in person. You get a written quote, an honest read on the slab, and a clear timeline based on the season.

Four seasons, four ways a floor gets tested

Flagstaff's climate is the main reason floors fail here, and the main thing we plan around. Choose a season to see what it does to concrete and how we deal with it.

Snowmelt, cinders and cold slabs

Every car that comes home after a storm drops a load of slush onto the garage floor. That meltwater carries road cinders and deicer with it, and on bare concrete it soaks in, freezes overnight and slowly scales the surface. A sealed, textured coating keeps water on top where it can be squeegeed out the door, and the cinders stop grinding into the slab.

Winter is also the hardest time to install. Most coatings need the concrete to stay above a minimum temperature while they cure. In heated garages we can work year-round; in unheated ones we either bring in temporary heat or plan for a milder stretch.

Wind, mud and the thaw

Spring in Flagstaff is windy and messy. Snowbanks melt into driveways, dirt roads turn to mud, and fine dust blows under every door. Garages and mudrooms take the brunt of it.

It is also when the damage from winter shows. Pitting, flaking and white salt stains appear on uncoated slabs once the snow is gone. If you are noticing that now, spring is a good time to book an estimate so your floor is coated before next winter.

High-country sun and monsoon rain

Summer is our busiest install season, with mild temperatures that suit most coating systems. But do not let the cool air fool you: at nearly 7,000 feet the sun is intense. Standard epoxies left in direct light, such as near a garage door or on a deck, can discolor. We use UV-stable topcoats wherever sunlight reaches.

Then the monsoon arrives, usually around July, bringing heavy afternoon storms. Outdoor projects get scheduled for mornings and watched closely against the forecast.

The window before the first snow

Fall brings crisp, dry days that are ideal for coatings, and it is the last good chance to protect a floor before winter. Many homeowners book now so their garage is sealed before the first storm, and cabin owners use the shoulder season to finish projects while the place is empty.

Overnight lows drop quickly in October and November, so we keep a close eye on slab temperature and favor fast-curing systems late in the season.

Garage floors that shrug off a Flagstaff winter

The garage is where winter comes indoors. Snow packed into wheel wells melts into puddles, and those puddles carry cinders, grit and deicing chemicals. On bare concrete, the cycle of soaking, freezing and thawing gradually breaks down the surface, which is why so many local garages have pitted, flaky patches near where the cars park.

A coating stops that cycle. The slab is ground, the scaling is repaired, and a sealed, textured surface goes over the top so water stays where you can push it out. We pick systems that tolerate cold slabs and resist deicer, then finish with a UV-stable topcoat so the strip of floor by the door does not fade in summer.

Residential epoxy for mudrooms, basements and cabins

Mountain living is hard on floors. Boots, skis, wet dogs, firewood and the occasional muddy bike all come through the door, and the rooms that take the abuse are often on a concrete slab: mudrooms, basements, laundry rooms, workshops and the lower level of many hillside homes.

A residential coating gives those spaces a seamless, washable surface that does not mind water or grit. It can be practical and simple, like a satin solid color in a laundry room, or a design feature, like a metallic finish in a finished basement. Moisture testing matters even more indoors, since some older Flagstaff slabs were poured without a good vapor barrier. Learn more on our residential epoxy flooring page.

Our flooring installation process

Most residential garages are finished in one to three days, depending on repairs and temperature. Here is how a project moves from first call to first parked car.

  1. Site visit and slab check

    We measure, look for scaling and cracks, test for moisture, check whether the space is heated and talk through how you use it.

  2. Written estimate and scheduling

    You get options with prices and a recommended install window based on the season and the system.

  3. Grinding and repairs

    Dust-controlled diamond grinding, then pit, spall and crack repairs so the surface is sound and even.

  4. Coating layers

    Primer or base coat, flake or color layers, and a protective topcoat with texture where it is needed.

  5. Cure, walkthrough and care plan

    We confirm cure times for the current temperature, walk the floor with you and leave written winter-care instructions.

Frequently asked questions

What Flagstaff homeowners and business owners ask us most. Still wondering about something? Call (928) 555-0177.

Can you install a floor coating in the winter?

Yes, with planning. In heated garages and interior rooms we install year-round. Unheated spaces need the slab and air kept above the product minimum while it cures, which we handle with temporary heat or by choosing a cold-tolerant system such as polyaspartic. We will be upfront if waiting a few weeks would give you a better result.

Will deicer and road cinders damage the coating?

A properly built system with a chemical-resistant topcoat handles deicer and meltwater far better than bare concrete. Cinders are abrasive, so we recommend a flake or textured finish that hides wear and a mat or squeegee routine in the worst months.

My garage floor is flaking and pitted. Can it be coated?

Usually, yes. That surface damage is called scaling and is common here. We grind away the loose material, fill the pits with a repair mortar or resin and then coat. Severe cases add some repair time, which we price in the estimate.

Is a coated floor slippery with snow and ice on it?

Any floor is slick with ice on it, but texture makes a big difference with water and slush. We add slip-resistant aggregate to topcoats in garages and entries, and flake floors have natural grip.

Do you work on cabins and second homes?

Often. Many cabin owners in Munds Park, Kachina Village and around Flagstaff schedule work while they are away. We can coordinate access, send photos as the work progresses and confirm cure times before you return.

How long until I can park in the garage?

Fast-cure systems are often ready for vehicles in a day or two in mild weather. Colder temperatures slow curing, so winter projects may need extra time. Your written estimate lists the expected times for your floor and season.

What does a garage floor coating cost in Flagstaff?

It depends on size, slab condition, the amount of scaling or crack repair and the system you choose. Because winter damage varies so much from garage to garage, we price each floor after seeing it, and you get a written estimate with options.
