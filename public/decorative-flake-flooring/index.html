Decorative flake floors with a high-country palette

Flake floors are the practical favorite in Flagstaff. The speckled texture hides cinders, mud and dust, gives grip when the floor is wet and can take on colors pulled straight from the landscape.

If you live with a gravel driveway, a dog or a winter's worth of road cinders, you already know that a plain floor shows everything. A flake floor does not. Its multi-colored speckle breaks up the surface so that a scatter of grit or a dusty footprint simply blends in.

That makes flake the most requested finish we install in Flagstaff garages and mudrooms. It is also more versatile than people assume. Change the chip size, the color blend and how densely the chips are spread, and the same system can look rugged and outdoorsy or refined enough for a finished basement.

How a flake floor is put together

A flake floor is several layers working together. First, the concrete is diamond-ground and any damage repaired. Next comes a pigmented base coat. While that base is still wet, we throw colored vinyl chips across it by hand in overlapping passes, so they settle evenly into the resin.

After the base cures, we scrape the surface to knock down any chips standing on edge and vacuum the excess. Then the clear topcoat goes on. That topcoat is the wearing surface: it seals the chips in, sets the sheen and provides the resistance to chemicals, salt and sunlight.

The chips are decorative rather than structural, but they do a lot of practical work. They add texture for traction, add a little thickness and hide the kinds of minor marks that make a solid-color floor look tired quickly.

Blends inspired by the landscape

You can choose from dozens of stock blends, but these color families tend to look right in Flagstaff homes and hide local dirt well.

Cinder and basalt

Charcoal, grey and a touch of rust red, echoing the volcanic cinders around town. Hides road grit better than anything.

Ponderosa

Tan, cinnamon and cream, like the bark of the pines. Warm and welcoming in mudrooms and garages attached to wood-trimmed homes.

Granite peak

Light grey, white and black. Bright and clean, good for basements and shops that need reflected light.

Meadow

Muted greens, greys and beige for a softer, natural look in entries and studios.

The base coat color matters too. We usually tint it to match the dominant chip color so the floor reads as one surface, even in places where the broadcast is a little lighter.

Choosing chip size and coverage

Size and coverage change the character of the floor more than most people expect.

OptionLookBest for
1/4" chips, full broadcastBold, textured, granite-likeGarages, shops, heavy-use entries
1/8" chips, full broadcastFiner speckle, more refinedMudrooms, smaller garages, laundry rooms
1/4" chips, partial broadcastSolid base with scattered chipsWorkshops and studios with a cleaner look
Micro chipsStone texture, very subtleBasements and interior living areas

Full broadcast hides the most dirt and gives the deepest texture. Partial broadcast shows more of the base color between chips, which some people prefer for a modern look, though it reveals a little more grit.

What a flake floor does better than other finishes

Camouflage. Cinders, pine needles, dog hair and dried mud all disappear into the speckle between cleanings.

Traction. The chip layer adds texture even before any slip-resistant additive. With one, the floor has good grip when wet with snowmelt.

Forgiveness. Repairs, old patches and minor unevenness in the slab are much less visible under flakes than under a solid color.

Impact tolerance. The extra build of the chip layer helps absorb the occasional dropped tool or ski without leaving a mark.

Character. It looks like a finished floor rather than painted concrete, and it suits the rugged style of many mountain homes.

Our installation process

  1. Prep and repair

    Diamond grinding, then repair of scaling, pits and cracks so the surface is sound.

  2. Base coat

    A pigmented base tinted to suit your blend, applied in manageable sections.

  3. Broadcast

    Chips thrown by hand to even coverage, or to your chosen density for partial broadcast.

  4. Scrape and vacuum

    After cure, loose and raised chips are removed for a smooth, consistent surface.

  5. Topcoat

    A clear protective topcoat, usually with anti-slip aggregate for Flagstaff floors.

Flake floors in a cold, gritty climate

Cinders are the defining feature of a Flagstaff garage in winter. They are abrasive and dark, and they end up everywhere. A dark or mid-tone flake blend hides them; a pale solid floor does not. That alone is why we recommend flake so often here.

Temperature affects installation. Base coats thicken in a cold garage, which changes how chips settle. We warm the space and material when needed and choose products that perform at lower temperatures.

For floors near open doors or on covered porches, UV exposure at altitude is strong. We use UV-stable topcoats so the chips and clear coat keep their color.

The craft behind a good flake floor

Two flake floors made with the same products can look completely different depending on who installed them. Even broadcasting takes practice: uneven throws leave thin spots and clumps that are permanent. The topcoat has to be thick enough to smooth the sharp chip edges without becoming slippery. Edges at doors, stem walls and thresholds need careful hand work. And in a cold garage, the timing of every step shifts. These details are what you are paying a professional crew for.

Keeping a flake floor looking good

Sweep or dust mop regularly, especially when cinders are on the roads. A soft-bristle deck brush and warm water with a neutral cleaner handle deeper cleaning. Rinse deicer residue now and then through winter. Avoid strong solvents and citrus degreasers on the topcoat.

After several years of heavy use, the topcoat may lose some sheen in the main traffic paths. A light sanding and fresh topcoat restores it without disturbing the chips underneath.

What affects the cost of a flake floor

Square footage sets the starting point, but slab condition usually moves the price most. Winter scaling, pitting, old paint and cracks all add grinding and repair time. Full broadcast uses more chips than partial broadcast, and custom blends cost more than stock ones. Smaller add-ons include coating stem walls and adding extra anti-slip aggregate for entries. Your estimate itemizes all of it, so you can choose where to spend.

Flake floor questions

Which flake blend hides cinders best?

Mid-tone and darker blends with charcoal, grey and some rust or brown hide cinders best. Very light blends look bright but show dark grit more.

Do flake floors chip or peel?

The chips are sealed under the topcoat, so they do not wear off. Peeling only happens when prep or moisture issues are ignored, which is why we grind and test every slab.

Can I have a custom blend?

It is possible to order a mix made to your own color choices, but lead times are longer. In practice, seeing a dozen stock blends on your actual floor usually turns up one that fits.

Is the surface rough on bare feet?

Not once the clear coat is on. Underfoot it has a light, even grain, enough to grip in socks or wet boots but smooth enough that a mop glides across it.

Can flake go on a covered porch?

Yes, with an exterior-rated, UV-stable topcoat and texture for wet conditions. Uncovered surfaces may suit a different system; see our patio coatings page.

How long does installation take?

A typical garage takes one to two days, plus cure time that depends on temperature. Mudrooms and smaller rooms are often faster.

Will a flake floor hide salt stains?

Salt residue sits on top of the sealed surface rather than soaking in, so it wipes off. Between cleanings, a mid-tone blend disguises light residue far better than a plain grey floor.

Can flake floors be recoated later?

Yes. When the clear topcoat dulls after years of use, it can be lightly sanded and recoated. The chip layer stays protected underneath, so the floor looks refreshed without a full redo.

Do you coat the floor under a workbench?

If it can be moved, yes. Fixed benches and cabinets can be coated around neatly. We will discuss which approach makes sense during the estimate.
